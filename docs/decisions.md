# Decisions

One entry per choice between options: what was decided, why, and what was rejected. Newest first. Earlier entries are reconstructed from code comments and project notes; dates are when the choice took effect.

### 2026-11-01: Pages next to a good page join the pool and score higher
The pool was the pages whose words match the attribute's labels, plus the pages the report's own index names and the ones carrying the company's remembered line. A table that runs on to the next page, or a figure on the page after the one the index names, carried no label and was never seen (the 1 Nov report counted "label on the next or previous page" among the misses). Chosen: fetch the page before and after each of the 10 best pages, score them like the rest, and add to every page a share of its best neighbour's score (NEIGHBOUR_WEIGHT 0.5; the whole share one page away, 0.4 two pages away). Rejected: giving the model page pairs for every candidate (doubles the tokens for every answer, most of which need one page); a decay over the whole document (what is 30 pages away is not more alike, and the scores would smear). The weights are first guesses, to be set by the evaluator; the shares are of the best score in the pool and taken from the scores before the step, so nothing travels further than two pages.

### 2026-11-01: Industry and region as fields on companies, loaded from the SDG2000 list
The reader's accuracy differs by country (India 41%, Japan 64%, China 88%) and industry (chemicals 47%, utilities 88%), and the next steps (scale rules by country, a memory shared by companies of one industry) need those facts on every company. Chosen: three text fields on `companies` (wba_industry, isic_code, hq_region) loaded by a re-runnable script from the list committed in `data/`. Rejected: a lookup to a new companies-master table (one more table to keep in step, for three values that change once a year); reading the CSV at run time in each script (the fields are then invisible in Airtable views and filters). The script never overwrites hq_country, ownership or isin: those may carry hand corrections.

### 2026-11-01: A record of what the model reader read, so a stopped run is carried on
The 1 Nov run (2022 to 2025, 8 shards) stopped at the 5-hour limit in every shard, and the reader always starts from the first company of its slice, so a second run would read the same companies again. Chosen: a `model_reads` table (company, attribute, year, when, found) and a skip of what was read in the last 30 days. Rejected: skipping years that already have a model reader row in attribute_values (a read that found nothing leaves no row, so those would be read again every run, and they are the costly ones); starting each run at a random company (no guarantee of covering the whole list). 30 days lets a run carry on for a month; `--fresh-days 0` reads everything again after a change to the reader.

### 2026-10-31: A Scope 2 figure with no basis goes under location-based, once
Many reports print one Scope 2 figure without saying location- or market-based. The reader proposed it under both attributes, and the reviewer rejected the market-based copy: we cannot know which it is. Chosen: file it under location-based only (the GHG Protocol's default method, and what a company reports when it buys no contractual instruments), say so in `detail`, and leave market-based empty. Rejected: under both (a duplicate a reviewer has to reject every time); under neither (loses a figure that is usually the location-based one). The reader report measures the cost on the market-based answers; if the validated answers file such figures under market-based too, the rule moves there.

### 2026-10-31: The report's own content index as the first signal that is not a label
The page ranking combined six signals from the page itself (label match, rule candidate, table-ness, year, label hits, document weight), and the pages it missed mostly carried no label at all. The GRI and ESRS content index, and the table of contents, are other pages of the same report saying where the figure is, the way early web search read the text of the links pointing at a page. The index rows are matched on the attribute's disclosure codes and words from `attributes.json`, the printed pages they cite are mapped to PDF pages with an offset estimated from the document's own page numbers, and those pages join the pool with a fixed boost. Chosen over learning the weights from the validated answers (next, once the reviewers' decisions add to them) and over reading the index with a model (a regular table; rules read it for nothing). Rejected: trusting the printed page number as the PDF page (covers and inside pages shift it by 0 to 4 pages).

### 2026-10-31: One model call per company, attribute and report, for every year it prints
The model reader asked for one year per call, although the table it reads prints two to five years side by side, so the history cost one call per year (a 2024 and 2025 run on 167 companies took five hours and $1). The model now returns every reporting year the pages print, each entry checked on its own page and against its column heading (a "2023" column cannot be reporting year 2024; "FY25" may be 2025 or 2024). Years are read newest first, and a year already returned as a comparison column is not read again. What is given up: the figure for 2024 may come from the 2025 report's comparison column rather than the 2024 report, and a restated figure differs from the one first reported (the validated answers are usually the latter); `detail` says which report it came from, and the Generic reader report measures how often those comparison figures match the validated answers, which decides whether a direct read of earlier years is worth paying for. Measured on the 31 Oct 2026 run (4,044 model rows, 809 with a validated answer): a figure read from the year's own documents matched 76% of the time (186 of 245), a comparison figure from the next year's report 66% (317 of 478), from two years later 61% (48 of 79). Every version is kept, not only the one shown: in the evidence database (`value_versions`), not in attribute_values, so the analysts review one value per company, attribute and year and see the others as a note (`other_reports`, `restated`). A company with five different Scope 1 figures for 2023 says something about its reporting, and the table lets that be measured. Rejected: one attribute_values row per version (four or five times the rows for the analysts, and the Disclosure Library's 500,000-record cap), and the existing `datapoints` table (meant for the one final value, with no deterministic key). So since 1 Nov 2026 every year asked is read and its own figure wins; comparison figures are kept for years not asked or where the year's read finds nothing. The cost is about one more call per company, attribute and year asked. Rejected: one call for all attributes of a company at once, because retrieval is per attribute (different pages hold emissions and revenue) and a longer reply is cut more often.

### 2026-10-31: Parallel jobs for the model reader, each a fixed slice of the companies
All 2,000 companies take about 25 hours of reading at one call per answer. The run can be split into up to 8 jobs that each take a fixed slice of the companies (by a hash of the id, so a company always lands in the same slice), write their own rows (ids are deterministic, so no duplicates) and pace their writes so that together they stay within the base's five requests a second. Rejected: one job over several days (GitHub's six-hour limit) and a queue in the database (more moving parts than a slice).

### 2026-10-31: Stock exchanges as a gap filler, Hong Kong and mainland China first
About 1 in 5 companies block automated access to their sites, many of them Chinese, and the analysts found reports (Transsion) only on an exchange. Listed companies must file their annual report with the exchange, whose databases are open: HKEXnews and cninfo (Shanghai and Shenzhen) first, because they cover the largest blocked group. Same rule as the SEC and ESEF: added only for a fiscal year with no annual report of the company's own in the library, so the record count stays low. Rejected for now: Japan (EDINET, an API key), Korea (DART, a key), India (BSE); next in line if these two work.

### 2026-10-30: Regulator filings are read as web pages, not printed to PDF
The 655 SEC and ESEF filings in report_library had no text. Printing them to PDF (as capture_pages.py does for web pages) was rejected: a 10-K prints to roughly 5 to 15 MB, and a file we make ourselves can only reach Airtable through the direct upload, which takes 5 MB at most (files Airtable copies from a public address can be far bigger, which is why larger reports are attached). The filing's own HTML marks every printed page, so `web_filing.py` splits it there and the text store gets the same pages the SEC or ESEF viewer shows; the row keeps the official address and no file.

### 2026-10-30: No learned vocabulary in retrieval (#145 closed)
`vocabulary.json` (#142) added a second search with the 12 strongest learned terms per attribute, meant to reach pages that print the value with no label. Scored on the same 200 answers, the share with the number on the ranked pages barely moved (revenue 55 to 57 %, Scope 1 72 %, Scope 2 location 42 %, market 50 to 48 %, capex 25 %), and the scoring run took twice as long. Not merged; the missed capex pages need another approach (the EU Taxonomy tables split the figure over rows).

### 2026-10-30: Revenue from a filing is its largest revenue concept
The revenue concepts were tried in a fixed order (`us-gaap:Revenues`, then contract revenue excluding and including assessed tax, net sales, revenue net of interest expense). In the first analyst review of `attribute_values`, 4 of the 5 SEC rows checked were rejected because the order picked a narrower line than WBA's revenue: net sales after excise taxes, contract revenue without other revenue, a card issuer's contract revenue instead of its revenue net of interest expense. The narrower concepts are always parts of the total, so `sec_facts.choose` now takes the largest the filing reports for the year (each concept as first filed; equal values keep the order). Applies to ESEF too, which uses the same choice. Rejected: reordering the list, because the right total sits in a different concept from one company to the next. Not solved by this: a total printed only in a company's own extension concept (gross sales before royalties, total revenue including other revenue in some IFRS reports, revenue including joint ventures).

### 2026-10-23: A browser on the owner's computer for refused sites
Plain requests from the owner's computer reached 11 of 30 refused recent reports; most of the rest check for a real browser. The owner decided to try a second pass in Microsoft Edge without a window (the base is a prototype and the reports are public). Safeguards: paths that robots.txt disallows are skipped; nothing clicks, types or solves, so captchas and logins stay refused; one page at a time with a pause; it runs only on the owner's computer. Rejected: solving captchas or using captcha services; paid proxies or rotating addresses.

### 2026-10-23: Refused files are fetched from the owner's computer
Sites that refuse our robot do so by network address: from GitHub's cloud servers a browser-like request and Internet Archive copies (also at other addresses of the same file) recovered 0 of 61 recent sustainability and annual reports (#53, closed without merging). The robot therefore also runs on the owner's Windows computer as a GitHub self-hosted runner, attached only to the private repository wba-text-store, where the workflow and the Airtable token live; it runs this repository's `pc_fetch.py` from main every 4 hours, every day (the owner's choice: the computer may be off; at most one run waits), up to 300 refused files and 150 refused report pages a run, 90 minutes. Reading the refused pages extends it beyond the 3,000 known files: reports on the roughly 340 sites that refuse us entirely become visible. A second computer can join with its own runner. Approved by the owner on 23 Oct. Rejected: attaching the runner to this public repository (any pull request could run code on the computer); paid proxies or rotating residential addresses (getting around a refusal rather than reading as an ordinary visitor would); running the runner as a Windows service (the service account does not see the user's Python).

### 2026-10-23: Disclosure completeness and page completeness, separately
The six completeness checks mix two different things: what the company discloses and we hold (four document checks) and whether we watch its report pages (two page checks, the health of our collection). A company can hold every report while we lose its page, and the other way round. Two scores now sit beside the combined one: `disclosure_completeness` and `page_completeness`, with daily averages in `base_health`. The combined `completeness` is kept unchanged so its history stays comparable. Suggested by the owner. Rejected: replacing `completeness` with the disclosure score (breaks the trend line and every view built on it).

### 2026-10-23: Page checks count only live pages (completeness rules v3, approved)
Under v2 the report hub and sustainability page checks pass when the company has any page of that type, even one unticked (monitoring switched off as junk) or gone (404/410). That flatters completeness: a company whose only reports page disappeared still shows the check as passed. v3 counts only pages still watched that have not gone. Measured with `company_coverage.py --compare --compare-rules v2,v3` on the pull request; measured on 23 Oct: average completeness 79.5% to 78.4%, 80 companies lose the report hub check and 52 the sustainability page check, the disclosure checks do not change. Approved by the owner the same evening and switched on (COVERAGE_RULES=v2 restores the old page checks). Gone pages are replaced nightly since #54, so most companies keep the check through the replacement. Rejected: counting blocked pages (403) as failed (the page is right, only our robot is refused).

### 2026-10-23: Non-English documents typed from their cover title
About 30,000 documents had no type: the Airtable AI field types English documents only, and their address said nothing. They did not count for completeness. A dictionary of cover-title phrases in a dozen languages (`cover_terms.json`: Portuguese, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, Arabic) now types them when the address and the file name say nothing; the phrase that appears first on the cover wins (the title comes first). Measured on the pull request by `year_eval.py` against the sure true types of non-English documents (mostly the analysts' Source Library labels). Rejected: switching the Airtable AI field on for every language (about 30,000 AI runs, an Airtable credit cost, and its accuracy on these languages unmeasured); machine translation of every cover (cost, and the title phrase is what decides).

### 2026-10-23: Clear page matches are added without a person
Suggested pages waited in `suggested_links` until a person ticked `approve_suggestions`, and report pages that started answering 404 stayed monitored, read every night for nothing. Nobody reviews suggestions reliably, so `discover_pages.py` now adds a candidate on its own when it is a clear best match: the only candidate of its type with 3 or more document links, or one with at least twice the document links of the next. It goes through the same path as an approval (`inbox.py`: deduplicated, deleted pages never come back, scanned within minutes). Gone pages are searched for first; the gone page is unticked with a dated note saying what replaced it. Ambiguous candidates still wait as suggestions. Rejected: adding every candidate (more records and wrong pages harvesting the wrong documents); keeping approval by a person (it does not happen). Found on the way: the page checks of completeness count a page of the type even when it is unticked or gone; to be measured and fixed separately.

### 2026-10-23: Claude labels the daily cards; the owner checks about 5
The daily cards asked the owner to classify documents from scratch into 24 types. He is not a document-type specialist and asked Claude about almost every card, so his labels added little the pre-read had not already said. Now Claude (Opus, the strongest model) labels every card with a confidence level and the quoted evidence; the owner checks only the cards where a person adds something: low or medium confidence, high-stakes disagreements with the pipeline, and 2 random high-confidence cards a day that measure Claude itself. One click per card (Agree, Disagree, Can't tell); cards left unanswered for 3 days settle with Claude's answer, so nothing depends on the owner's time. Labels keep `labelled_by` (Edi, Claude, source_library, correction), and Claude's labels are never used to score the Airtable AI type, to avoid one model grading another; the Source Library 2025 labels remain the independent truth for that. The run counts against the owner's Claude plan, not extra spend. Rejected: keeping the owner as the first labeller (slow, and less accurate than the model on type distinctions); letting Claude label without any human audit (its error rate would be unknown).

### 2026-10-23: The owner does not work through failure rows
Pipeline QA held about 36,000 open failures on its first day, with one person to act. The first design said a person acts on PAGE_BLOCKED, PAGE_GONE, PAGE_WRONG_TYPE and unresolved CO_GAP rows; the owner pointed out that this would never happen at that scale. Now: the weekly review fixes gone and wrongly typed pages itself in the Disclosure Library (new address into `add_link`, page `type`, `monitor`), the daily gap research works on CO_GAP, the robot retries blocked sites, and the owner acts only on analyst flags and on at most 5 items a week the Monday notification lists ("Para ti esta semana"). `who_fixes` says "Not you" on every other code. Card instructions changed the same day: `true_type` and `true_year` on every card (the year measurement counts only cards with `true_year`), `verdict` optional. Every code now has an automatic owner, a script or a scheduled Claude task; where it is not built yet (blocked sites, scanned PDFs, non-English typing, failed-job re-runs, gone-page replacement), the gap is listed in quality.md and the failures stay counted until it is. Analyst flags are a bonus signal; nothing depends on analysts reviewing. Rejected: a daily triage of Open failures by the owner (one person cannot keep up, and most rows are closed by one code fix); relying on analysts to review (they may not).

### 2026-10-23: Completeness rules v2, annual reports that carry the sustainability disclosure
Two gaps in the checks. (1) The type "Registration Document" holds 2,746 documents: 1,157 under 20 pages, almost all bond final terms, pricing supplements and prospectus supplements, but also the universal registration documents that ARE the annual report in France, Spain and Belgium (AXA, Sodexo, TotalEnergies, Eiffage, BBVA); none counted for the annual check. (2) Since CSRD, BRSR and the Hong Kong ESG rules, many companies publish the sustainability disclosure inside the annual report; 117 of the 530 companies without a 2024+ sustainability report hold a 2024+ whole annual report, 10-K or URD classed E embedded. v2: a URD (title names a registration document or annual (financial) report, 100+ pages, no prospectus or debt words) passes the annual check; a whole annual report, 10-K or URD classed E embedded passes the sustainability check when its sustainability section is a run of 8+ pages or its contents name a sustainability statement or BRSR (approved by the owner on 23 Oct after the hand check: blocks of 3-7 pages, e.g. a CSR chapter, did not amount to reporting sustainability). `company_coverage.py --compare` measures it (average, companies that change, the documents that make them change, a hand-check sample); Approved by the owner on 23 Oct with the 8-page threshold; v2 is the default from #49 (COVERAGE_RULES=v1 restores the old checks), and `companies.sust_reporting` says dedicated / embedded / both / none. Rejected: adding the whole Registration Document type to the annual family (bond documents would pass the check); counting any annual report for sustainability (financial-only reports would pass); retyping annual reports with embedded sections as Integrated Report (the type is the analysts' label and must stay comparable with the Source Library).

### 2026-10-23: House rules for human rights documents
A modern slavery statement (UK and Australian Modern Slavery Acts; also "human rights and modern slavery statement", "slavery and human trafficking statement") is an annual report on forced-labour risk in operations and supply chain, not a policy: type **Social Reports**. A human rights statement or commitment without modern slavery ("Statement on Human Rights") is the company's human rights policy under another name: **Human Rights Policy**. A "modern slavery policy" stays Human Rights Policy. Reason: the Human Rights Policy check asks whether the company has a human rights commitment; a statement required by law would otherwise pass it. The analysts' short type list put most of these under Policy Documents; `sl_labels.py` applies the house rules to the labels it writes (with the rule in `reason`) and never writes to the Source Library. Rejected: following the analysts' Policy Documents (a catch-all that hides what the document is); counting modern slavery statements as Human Rights Policy (inflates the check).

### 2026-10-23: Files too big to attach are read from the company's address
Airtable attaches a file either by fetching its URL itself (many company sites refuse Airtable's servers) or through the upload endpoint, which takes at most 5 MB. A PDF over 5 MB that Airtable could not fetch got `file_status = too_big` and was never read: no first pages, no type, no year, no full text. On 23 October that was 2,996 rows, 1,690 of them untyped and 1,221 dated 2023+, across 215 companies; the biggest files are the full annual, integrated and sustainability reports (CLP's 256-page 2025 annual report showed as "Document 2025"). `build_cover_text.py` and `build_fulltext.py` now read such rows straight from `document_url` (up to 150 MB), so they get first pages, AI type, year and full text like any other; only the attachment stays empty (open the link instead). Rejected: compressing or splitting PDFs to fit 5 MB (changes the file, loses pages); hosting copies elsewhere for Airtable to fetch (storage, and a public copy of every report).

### 2026-10-23: Year rule changes are measured before they are written
On 22 October two bulk rewrites of document years (first-pages fiscal years for 41,697 documents; the doc_year rules over the whole library) ran between two nightly completeness runs, and average completeness fell from 80.0% to 78.9% the next morning: some of the new years were corrections, some were new errors (a 2024 quarterly release dated 2023 because it quotes the previous Form 10-K; a 2024 annual report excerpt dated 2015 from a ten-year table). Three rules changed: a comparison period ("the preceding quarter ended ...") is not evidence; a title on the first pages two or more years away from the decided year costs 30 points (a report is titled by its period or the year after, never two years apart), so the medium-confidence text year drops below 60 and the title rule decides; a run of three or more years at the top of a cover is a table header, not a title. Every change to the year rules now runs `year_eval.py` on the pull request (main vs branch against every sure true year) and is merged only when it fixes more than it breaks; bulk rewrites follow the merge, not the other way round. Measured on the PR: the stored years scored 83.0% against 3,986 true years and the rules on main 83.4%, so the 22 October rewrites were net corrections and the completeness drop was mostly real. A first version also ignored periods quoted from another document ("our Annual Report on Form 10-K for the year ended ..."): it broke 11 years and fixed 3, because in US sustainability reports, CDP answers and proxy statements the 10-K cited is the reporting year itself; dropped. Rejected: letting any title year override the text (sustainability reports are often titled one year after the period they cover); reverting the rewrites (most of the changed years were corrections).

### 2026-10-22: A file named "financial statements" can be the whole annual report
In Italy ("Relazione finanziaria annuale"), Germany ("Jahresabschluss und Lagebericht") and the Nordics the statutory annual report is published under the name of its financial statements, but holds the management report, the corporate governance report and, from 2025, the sustainability statement (Acea 2025: 452 pages, file "2025-consolidated-financial-statements.pdf"). `doc_scope.py` now keeps such a file Full when its first pages list both a management report and the financial statements and the contents page numbers reach 70 to 105% of the file's length. Tested on the 487 "Part of a report" rows of 60+ pages: 7 change, all whole reports. Rejected: counting the parts named on the first pages without the page test (46 changes, most of them wrong: the sections of split UK reports carry the whole report's navigation bar); a page-count floor such as 150 pages (standalone financial statements volumes of banks run to 400 pages and are real parts).

### 2026-10-23: Sustainability class for documents the full text never reaches
After the first run about 110,000 rows were blank. Two fixes. (1) 10-Ks, universal registration documents and financial statements volumes are added to the full-text build (`CORE_TYPES`, about 14,000 documents, read over the next nights): registration documents carry France's CSRD statements and many "financial statements" files are whole statutory reports, so their class needs the pages. (2) Interim reports, statutory documents and company presentations stay out of the full text (about 95,000 documents, little sustainability content); they are decided from their first pages: E when the contents names a sustainability section, else F, with the reason saying they are not read in full. Rows with no text at all get their class from the type when the type decides it (D, integrated E, S). Rejected: reading every type in full (doubles the store and the nightly build for documents that rarely hold a sustainability section); F for annual reports from the first pages alone (their sustainability section is often listed after page 6).

### 2026-10-23: Analysts flag, the owner corrects
Analysts in other teams use the reports and spot errors. They get an interface on the Disclosure Library itself (not on the synced Disclosures View base, whose tables are read-only copies: nothing written there reaches the pipeline). In it they can add links and files (through `inbox.py`, with every duplicate guard) and flag a document (`analyst_flag`: wrong type, wrong year, wrong company, only part of the report, duplicate, file broken, not a disclosure, other; plus `analyst_flag_note`). They cannot change type, title, year or discard. Why: a correction by the owner becomes a sure label in the golden set, so letting several people edit those fields directly would mix unreviewed opinions into the measurement. A flag is a pointer for the owner, recorded as a DOC_FLAGGED failure; the owner's fix is the label, and its reason records the flag. Rejected: letting analysts edit `source_type_check` and `curated_doc_title` (faster, but unreviewed labels); collecting notes in the Disclosures View base and importing them (one more token, up to an hour of delay, and notes rather than structured flags).

### 2026-10-22: Where the sustainability disclosure lives: a class next to the type
Since the 2024 reports many companies put the sustainability disclosure inside the annual report (EU CSRD sustainability statements, India's BRSR, Hong Kong ESG sections, UK TCFD chapters) and stopped the separate report. Typed "Annual Report", those documents did not count as sustainability reporting. `sustainability_class.py` adds a second fact, following the public four-class taxonomy of SustainabilityReports.com so the counts are comparable: D dedicated (standalone sustainability, ESG, CSR, impact reports), E embedded (integrated reports, and annual reports whose pages hold a substantive sustainability section), S specialized (climate, CDP, GRI, policies...), F financial only. Two things go further than that taxonomy: the standards named are a separate multi-select (`frameworks`: ESRS, GRI, SASB, ISSB, TCFD, TNFD, SDGs, Integrated Reporting Framework, EU Taxonomy, GHG Protocol, SBTi, CDP, UN Global Compact, BRSR, HKEX ESG Guide) instead of report types, and the embedded class is read from the pages in nine languages, with the evidence in `class_reason` and the share of sustainability pages in `sustainability_share`. A page counts as "about sustainability" at 1.5 lexicon terms per 1,000 characters and 3 different terms: on the 452 analyst-typed annual reports with full text, every block found in a hand check of 30 was a real sustainability, TCFD, ESRS or BRSR section, and risk-factor pages (which name each term once) stayed out. Rejected: counting pages by a flat term count (tables and photo pages of sustainability reports fail it, risk-factor pages pass it); deciding Integrated Reports from their pages (Japanese ones have thin or garbled English text; the type is the better evidence); an AI field (English only, no evidence, cannot be re-run from a workflow); writing a provisional F from the first pages alone (the contents page of a long annual report is often past page 6: the class stays blank until the full text is read).

### 2026-10-22: Document scope by rules, not the AI field
`scope` (Full document / Part of a report / Summary or short document) is decided by `doc_scope.py` from the file name (page ranges, chapter numbers, section words), the title, the page count and the first page, with the rule written in `scope_reason`. On 60 random report documents judged by hand the rules were right on 50 of 54 and the AI field `doc_scope` on 48 of 52; the AI field also leaves every non-English document blank and gives no reason. Rejected: keeping the AI field (no reason to check, English only, its 41+ page "sections" were often whole reports); a table-of-contents rule (figures on contents pages read as page numbers, tested and dropped).

### 2026-10-22: Years from the first pages when there is no full text
The full-text store holds about 42,000 documents (the report families); the other ~230,000 rows got their year only from the title or the link. The same reader now runs on `cover_text` (the first six pages) for those rows. Checked on 1,351 documents with an analyst fiscal-year-end date, using the first pages alone: score 60-79 was 96% right and 80+ was 99%, the same calibration as the full text, so the same threshold (60) applies. Evidence from the first pages is prefixed "first pages:" and the document is read again from its full text once that exists. Rejected: extending the full-text store to every document first (weeks of extraction and gigabytes for documents whose first pages already state the period).

### 2026-10-22: The Source Library is the first ground truth, with doubt flags
The analysts' reviewed 2025 base gives type and year for thousands of documents the pipeline also holds; it is used as evaluation labels before any daily label exists. Analyst labels are not assumed perfect: where the pipeline has strong contrary evidence the label is kept out of the evaluation and shown to a person. Rejected: importing more Source Library documents (webpage captures and secondary types do not move completeness; the measurement does).

### 2026-10-22: Claude reviews a daily sample; Sonnet daily, Opus weekly
Claude pre-labels the 15 daily cases (type, year, title, with a reason) and the owner adjudicates; a weekly Opus review turns patterns into pull requests. Both run on the owner's Claude plan, not the API. Rejected: Claude reviewing every new document (cost, and it would replace measurement with a second unmeasured classifier); no human labels at all (nothing to measure against). Agreement-control cases are shown without a suggestion so the accuracy measurement is not anchored.

### 2026-10-22: Separate Pipeline QA base
Failures, runs, labels and evals live in their own base, keyed so a recurring failure is one row. Rejected: logging inside the Disclosure Library (500,000-record cap, and the log would grow with every run); GitHub logs (expire after 90 days, cannot be compared across commits).

### 2026-10-22: Disagreements matter only when they move a completeness check
URL type and AI type disagree on half the English corpus. A disagreement is a failure only when choosing one type over the other changes a completeness check (sustainability family, annual family, Code of Conduct, Human Rights Policy), and a written row only for report-shaped 2023+ documents. Rejected: treating every disagreement as a failure (40,000 low-stakes ones, such as Financial Statement vs Interim Reports, change nothing).

### 2026-10-21: One year field, fiscal-year convention
`year` is the year in which the reporting period ends (the Source Library convention, 97% of analyst records), read from the full text with a confidence score; the AI year counts only when its quoted evidence is found in the document. Rejected: the year in the file name or upload folder (systematically off by one for reports published the following year).

### 2026-10-21: Import from the Source Library, never write to it
Analyst-curated reports 2023+ and missing policies are copied into the Disclosure Library with duplicate guards; the Source Library is read with a separate read-only token. Rejected: syncing both ways.

## 2026-10-26 How the evidence database is filled
The text store (private repository, gzipped page files) stays the source; Supabase is a copy that can be rebuilt from it. `load_supabase.py` loads only files it has not loaded before (table `load_log`), oldest first, so a run can stop anywhere and the next continues. For each document only its newest text is copied, and its earlier pages are replaced, because a re-extracted document may have fewer pages; this replaces derived rows in the copy and deletes nothing in Airtable or the store. Every document the store has tried gets a `documents` row, failures included (text_status), so coverage gaps are visible in the database too. The word index uses Postgres's 'simple' configuration (no stemming): the corpus is in many languages, and figures, acronyms and names must match as written; it reads the first 200,000 characters of a page and is built once after the first big load, which is much faster than updating it row by row. Extracted values get their own table (`datapoints`) with the value as printed and normalized, unit, period, scope, document, page, quote, and whether it was stated or calculated (the golden dataset holds calculated answers, such as a board share worked out from "1 of 12"). Loading stops at SUPABASE_MAX_GB (repository variable, 6 GB when unset): a Supabase disk that fills puts the database in read-only mode, and automatic disk growth is too slow for a bulk load. Rejected: loading from Airtable (no full text there), stemmed English search (misses other languages), and building the index before the load.

## 2026-10-26 File metadata as a clue to the publication date, not as the date
Owner's question: do we look at the metadata of the files? Every PDF carries a creation and a modification date. They are recorded (`file_dates.py`, fields file_created, file_modified, file_dates_from) for the key report types from 2023, but they are noisy: the creation date is the day the final file was exported, usually days or weeks before the release, and the modification date moves whenever the file is re-saved or replaced, sometimes years later. So they will rank below every date the report states (regulator filing, a stated publication date, board approval) and are accepted only when they fall after the period end and before the robot first saw the file; they also serve to flag a stated date that is far from the file's own dates. Rejected: taking the creation date as the publication date (export, not release), and the web server's Last-Modified header (often the upload or the last server migration, and the attachment host does not give the company's).

## 2026-10-26 Searching the evidence database: through functions, WBA addresses only
The search page and Claude reach the text only through three database functions (`search_pages`, `search_summary`, `search_passages` in `supabase/search.sql`); the tables stay closed to the API. Each function first checks who is asking: a signed-in user whose address ends in @worldbenchmarkingalliance.org, an address the owner listed in `search_access`, or a direct database session (the loader, the checks, the SQL editor). Searches use the same 'simple' word index as the load (no stemming, every language, phrases in quotes, -word to exclude). A search looks at the first 2,000 matching pages before sorting newest first, and counts stop at 100,000 pages, so a common word answers in seconds instead of minutes. Rejected: opening the tables to signed-in users with row level security policies (any column and any query, including ones that scan millions of pages), and a domain allow-list in Supabase's sign-up settings alone (it does not protect the data if a sign-up slips through). Known gap: Chinese and Japanese text has no spaces, so the 'simple' index cannot split it into words; searching those documents needs a different index, to be decided when it matters.

## 2026-10-26 Phrase search: store the words, put the filters in the index
The first search functions (same day) re-read each candidate page's text to check a phrase, because the word index holds words but not their positions, and Postgres recomputes the words from the text for the check. For "scope 3" that is every page with both "scope" and "3": hundreds of thousands, at about 0.4 ms each, so the count ran past two minutes. Measured on a 1-in-11 copy of the corpus (607,081 pages) with the same memory settings as the Small compute: "scope 3" count 11.8 s, results 2.7 s; at full size about 11 times that. Chosen: keep each page's words with positions in a column (`pages.words`, the textbook Postgres layout for this), so the phrase check reads them instead of re-reading the text (0.05 ms a page): count 1.6 s, results 0.6 s on the copy, cold cache. And since every page fetched that a filter then throws away costs a disk read on a 2 GB machine, the document's year, type and company are copied onto the page and indexed together with the words (one GIN index with btree_gin): a search for "climate" in 2025 annual reports fetches only pages that match all three, 5 ms warm. Newest first is then a loop over years from the newest, stopping when enough rows are found, so a rare phrase and a common word cost about the same; counts stop at 20,000 pages. The copies are kept in step by two triggers (a page written gets its document's values; a document whose year, type or company changes updates its pages), so no writer can leave them stale. Cost: the words take about twice the space of the text (positions compress badly), about 21 GB at full size; the index with the three extra columns is 2 to 4 percent larger than one on the words alone. The disk goes to 100 GB, which the pre-2021 corpus needs anyway. The migration runs inside the 6-hourly load, in batches that continue across runs, and the search functions switch to the new index only when it exists, so the search keeps working on the old index meanwhile. Rejected: a lossless index with positions (RUM) and the PGroonga engine, both available on Supabase but neither testable here before touching the paid database; keeping the text-only index and bounding candidates (a common word in a phrase defeats any bound); sorting all matches by year (reads every match before showing one).

## 2026-10-26 Measure the evidence layer before any extraction model

## The company's memory: the line of another year's right answer (1 Nov 2026)

Companies print a figure on the same line from one report to the next. When the right value for another year is known (a validated answer now; accepted rows later), the page of that year's documents that prints it shows the line, the document type and the scale. `company_memory.py` keeps those for the nearest other year and uses them two ways: this year's pages that carry 80% of the line's words join the pool and get 1.0 on the page score, and the model reader is told how the company printed the figure that year. Not the value: the reader would anchor on it, and the year's own column is what is wanted. The year being read is never used, so the Generic reader's score stays honest. Chosen before learning general ranking weights from the right answers, because it needs no training set and the signal is the strongest we have per company; general learning from the reviewers' decisions comes next. First score (1 Nov 2026, 40 answers per attribute): revenue with a memory 9 of 9 on the ranked pages and 56% right against 61% and 45% without; capex 0 of 10 right with one (the line of another year's capex figure is rarely the line of this year's), so `"memory": false` switches it off per attribute. Scope 1, Scope 2 and employees got no memory: the validated answers hold one year for them. Their memory will come from accepted rows and from matched rows in `value_versions`.

The search index (`pages_search` on words, year, doc_type, wba_id) is not extended with library_id for this. The memory looks up pages by document and page number, which the primary key already covers, and searches within the company's documents by restricting to them first (as the capex query does since 31 Oct 2026). Rebuilding the index on every page of the store takes hours on Supabase (27 Oct 2026) and would not make these lookups faster.

## Web search for missing reports: Serper, on the company's own sites (1 Nov 2026)

When a company's report is not linked from the pages we watch, or its site refuses us, and no stock exchange filing has it, nothing brought it in. A web search does. Google's own Custom Search JSON API takes no new customers since 20 January 2026 and stops on 1 January 2027; what Google offers instead is limited to 50 domains (Programmable Search Element, Agent Search) or returns written answers, not results (grounding). Serper returns Google's results, takes `site:` and `filetype:pdf`, and costs about $1 per 1,000 searches ($50 minimum, credits expire after six months). It is unofficial (it reads Google's result pages; Google's case against SerpApi, a similar service, was dismissed in July 2026), so the provider sits behind one class (`Serper` in `web_search.py`) and can be swapped; Brave Search API ($5 per 1,000, its own index) is the fallback.

A result on the company's own sites (or a known document host, with the company's name) that names the report and a missing year is added on sight. Any other result that could be the report is opened and judged on its first pages: the company's name anywhere in the first 15 pages or the file's properties (a cover may carry only a logo), the report and a missing year in the first pages (deeper pages print every comparison year), and 8 pages or more. A search by name also finds news sites, aggregators and companies with similar names, so the address alone cannot be trusted, but a company's report on a CDN, a group site or an archive must not be lost either (the owner's call, 1 Nov 2026); the cover settles it. Up to 4 files are opened per company and family; what fails is listed in `web_search_note` with the reason. Documents go through `research_link` and the inbox, not straight into report_library, so the duplicate and tombstone guards, the attachment and the typing are the ones every other document gets. Rejected: a monthly sweep of every company (it searches companies that have their reports and spends credits for nothing), and adding results from any site.
